# Hypothesis H1 — Biography becomes causal

## Strong intuition

A historical subject is not merely a system that can retrieve a record of the past. Its past changes what it can or tends to do now.

## Minimal operational hypothesis

Let two systems begin from the same initial state S0 and share the same transition rule F.

They experience different event histories H_A and H_B.

Afterward they encounter the same context C.

If:

1. their resulting dispositions D_A and D_B differ,
2. the difference is reproducibly attributable to H_A vs H_B,
3. no earlier event was rewritten or deleted,
4. no fixed persona encoded the later difference in advance,

then history is causally relevant to present disposition.

This is **not** yet a claim of consciousness, subjective experience, or a physical field.

## Failure conditions

H1 fails for this implementation if:

- later behavior is fully determined by a fixed persona;
- history is only retrieved as text and then restated;
- changing event order has no effect where order should matter;
- deleting or rewriting history is required to revise a disposition;
- supposedly different histories converge only because the test has hard-coded the expected answer.

## Garden rule

Do not pull the grass to measure growth.

Observation should prefer externally visible trajectories and append-only traces over destructive introspection or reset-based ablation.
